Things might be changing for those who have actively running Shopify or any type of e-commerce store located in France that targets massive email lists with email marketing pixels, and I'm here to simplify it and share some checklists.

Email marketing and tracking are evolving as technology and methods advance. On the one side, compliance always has to evolve to keep up with that in order to maintain the security of digital ground.

The recommendation about e-privacy was published in April 2026 by the CNIL and is becoming necessary in just a few days.

Before:
1 - You were adding pixels by default in newsletters.
2 - You were able to bundle or imply the consent through a general newsletter sometimes.
3 - You didn't have to document the proof of consent.
4 - You were allowed to reuse legacy lists without much rechecking.
5 - You could make the withdrawal option not very visible or even hidden.
6 - You were able to add the open tracking method to emails to watch them silently.
7 - Email open rates were an important metric and the main source of reporting for you.
8 - You were able to define tracking only one time and leave it in place across all your campaigns.
9 - You were able to see compliance as a legal side task.

Starting from 14 July:
1 - You are going to define a clear legal purpose; if you're using it for marketing, a prior consent.
2 - You are going to treat consent specifically, explicitly, and strictly tied to the tracking purpose.
3 - You will make sure that you keep the proof, timestamps and collection contexts of each email with tracking pixels.
4 - Re-informing and reviewing the legacy contacts before contacting again will be a part of your routine.
5 - Email withdrawal must be easy and as simple as the consent mechanism; it's becoming a thing that you will have to re-implement.
6 - You must be aware that open tracking methods are consent-sensitive in most marketing cases
7 - You are going to change your vision to see Open rates as a primary KPI and move more toward clicks, conversions, revenue and replies.
8 - You should separate security, transactional, deliverability, and marketing emails; it's mandatory.
9 - Compliance is going to be the part of the email infrastructure itself, you should starting seeing it like that as well.

So what to do now? Let me present you a quick list:

Audit checklist for beginning:

- Check which of your emails contain pixels or tracking links. (Check it from your preferred email marketing CRM.)
- Whether the purpose is marketing or strictly necessary (just separate them into two).
- Check to see how you collect the consent, ideally at email capture. (Create and use a test email account for that, and check the CRM.)
- How the withdrawal button works in practice. (Important thing: It's different than an opt-out button, this button will revoke your clients' consent from email tracking.)
- Whether you can prove consent with timestamps and records. (Make sure all emails you send get archived automatically.)
- How legacy contacts are being informed and given an objection path. (create a specialized email just for this purpose with two buttons and a brief description)

That might seem like too much of a process for those who work without someone who takes care of the email marketing actively, but believe me, it's completely manageable.
